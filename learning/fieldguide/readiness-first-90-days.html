PART IX · CHAPTER 51

Readiness and the first 90 days

How to start a governance programme from low maturity: assess on ten dimensions using artefacts rather than interviews, build the seven controls every later control depends on, separate an interim state you can reach in 90 days from a target state you plan for, and sequence the first three months so the riskiest agents are safe in the first thirty.

51.1Start from where they are

Generic Most clients do not start with a blank page. They start with agents already running: pilots that became production, assistants with tool access, SaaS agents switched on by a business team, scripts calling models with a service account. The first job is not to design the target state; it is to find out what is running, decide what must change this month, and build the minimum that makes every later control possible.

Your build ARB's own history is a usable maturity scale. Its build story moved from unguarded to specified, enforced, verified, portable, fleet and finally operable and defensible. A client estate climbs the same ladder, more slowly and unevenly; the four levels below compress it.

Generic The smallest set of controls that makes every later control possible. Each item below is something a later control depends on, which is why it comes first.

51.5Sequencing the first 90 days

  1. Days 0–30, discover and stabilise. Pull inventories from every platform, interview owners, apply the tiering rule. For any agent that can already take irreversible or financial actions, put human approval and a tested kill switch in place now, before anything else is designed. Start logging effects, even crudely. Convene the governance board and start the rulings log with its first decisions: the tiering rule and the minimum controls.
  2. Days 31–60, enforce where it matters. Put a gateway in front of the few systems where agent effects matter most (payments, customer records, HR) and make it refuse unregistered agents. Stand up the evidence plane behind it. Publish the RACI, the waiver process and the intake form. Ship the first paved-road template with the gate as a required check, and release one agent through it.
  3. Days 61–90, prove and scale. Recertify the existing agents against the minimum controls. Red-team the highest-tier agents and turn findings into cases. Publish the first KPIs (lead time by tier, inventory completeness, open waivers), re-score readiness, and present the target-state plan with its dependencies and risks.

51.6Starting from shadow AI and SaaS agents

Generic Where many agents run inside SaaS products the client does not host, the minimum set changes shape. Inventory comes from each vendor's admin surfaces and identity logs (which agents hold tokens to what); enforcement moves to the boundary the client controls, a gateway in front of its own systems of record; and evidence for calls into those systems is the client's own, while evidence inside the SaaS product is the vendor's and should be labelled as such (Chapter 44). The single most useful finding in a shadow-AI discovery is usually the list of agents acting on a person's own credentials: each is an unowned identity with that person's full authority.

51.7What to say no to, for now

  • A complete catalog before any enforcement. Seven controls that work beat fifty on paper.
  • One platform for everything. Govern across what exists; standardise new builds on a paved road (Chapter 45).
  • Waiting for regulation. Transparency duties already apply, and the evidence you start collecting now is what any regime will ask for (Chapter 48).
  • Certification first. ISO/IEC 42001 certifies a management system; build one that operates before auditing it.
  • Buying a tool to be the programme. Tools fill rows in the target state; they do not decide owners, tiers or waivers (Chapter 46).

51.9In the room

In the room
  • "Where do we start?" "With what is already running. In the first month we find every agent, give each an owner and a risk tier, and put human approval and an off switch on any that can move money or change records irreversibly. Everything else builds on that."
  • "Governance will slow our teams down." "The first thing we build is a road that makes the governed way the fast way, and we measure lead time by risk tier so you can see it."
  • "Our vendor already covers this." "Inside their product, partly. Not across your other platforms, not with your business limits, and not with evidence you control. We use what they provide and add the checkpoint in front of your systems."
  • "Shouldn't we wait for the regulation to settle?" "Some of it already applies, and every regime will ask the same questions: what agents you have, who owns them, what stops them and what they did. We start collecting those answers now."
Waiting on the builds
