PART VII · CHAPTER 46

The independent tool landscape and build vs buy

Between the clouds and your own code sits a crowded market of independent tools: gateways, tracing, evaluation, guardrails, policy engines, identity, memory, sandboxes and harnesses. Many are open source, several were acquired in the last year, and most overlap with a cloud feature. This chapter maps them by component and gives a method for deciding, component by component, whether to build, adopt or buy, using your builds' own choices as the worked examples.

46.1Why an independent layer exists

Generic Independent tools survive next to the clouds for three reasons: they work across clouds and models (most enterprises run several), they move faster in narrow areas (evaluation, tracing, red teaming), and open-source ones can run inside the client's boundary. Their risks are the mirror image: integration work, vendor viability, and overlap with platform features that improve every quarter.

46.3Open-source harnesses

Generic Whole harnesses are also open source: OpenHands, Goose (Block, donated to the Agentic AI Foundation), opencode, Cline, Aider, Hermes Agent, and DeepSeek's harness preview. They matter for two reasons: they are executors a meta-harness may delegate to (Chapter 6), and they are the best place to study how real loops, permissions and sandboxes are built. The 2026 harness study found that production harnesses rarely import general frameworks and that skills had overtaken MCP as the extensibility mechanism (Chapter 1). Lab T19 runs one coding task through two of them.

46.4A consolidating market

Generic Acquisitions in 2025 and 2026 moved several independent tools inside larger platforms Verify each before quoting: ServiceNow bought Traceloop (the company behind OpenLLMetry) to observe agent reasoning in AI Control Tower; Snowflake bought Natoma for MCP; Check Point acquired Lakera; Palo Alto Networks acquired Protect AI; CoreWeave acquired Weights & Biases. The second prediction question: check data path and contract first (where your traces go now, under whose terms), then the product's roadmap and open-source licence, then your exit plan. A tool behind a seam with an open data format (OpenTelemetry, your own eval files) is cheap to leave; one without is not.

46.5Build, adopt or buy: a method

For each component, answer in order:

  1. Is it the control itself, or infrastructure for it? Controls whose semantics are yours (what a refund limit means, what evidence must show) are built or authored; engines and plumbing are adopted or bought.
  2. Does a native or available product meet the control's contract? Use the control's own objective, trigger, evidence and test; then run its falsifiers against the product (Chapter 24). "Has a feature called guardrails" is not an answer.
  3. Data path and residency: where does the data go, and can it run inside the client's boundary?
  4. Can it sit behind a seam? If replacing it would mean a rewrite, the bar rises sharply (Chapter 7).
  5. Total cost: licence plus integration plus operation plus the upgrades it will force.
  6. Maturity and viability: release cadence, users, maintainers, funding or owner.

The third prediction question follows from step 2: build when the available component does not meet the control's contract, or meets it only on one platform when you need it on several.

46.7Defaults for an AICoE

Assumption For a GCP-standard AICoE serving clients on several platforms:

  • Build: the catalog and its semantics, the enforcement plane's decision logic and PIP adapters, the evidence schema and chain, the gate and sign-off, the registry of record, parity and equivalence suites.
  • Adopt (open source): OpenTelemetry, OPA or Cedar, an MCP SDK, an evaluation framework whose files you own, a red-team tool.
  • Use native: agent identity, runtimes, sandboxes, model screening, gateways, on each platform where they meet the control contract.
  • Buy selectively: an observability and evaluation product if the team needs its workflow; an AI security posture product for estate discovery; always behind open formats.
