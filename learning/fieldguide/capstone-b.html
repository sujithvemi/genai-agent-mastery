Capstone B: a framework for a client estate

The design and build of a governance framework for an estate you do not own: an inventory and tiering model, a cut of the control catalog for platforms you cannot instrument, an enforcement-point map per control and platform, a crosswalk, an evidence design, an operating model with a rollout, and a toolkit with a conformance suite the client can run after you leave.

Predict first
  1. Thirty agents run across a CRM-native agent platform, Gemini Enterprise and a few custom ADK agents. Which of your controls cannot be enforced inside the CRM platform at all?

53.1What this capstone proves

Generic Capstone B is level GF-L3: designing and building the governance framework for a client's estate, not your own build. It is the work you will be paid for, so it doubles as GTM support-pack material. The scenario in learning/capstones/B_client_framework.md is generic and assumed, not a client's facts:

  • Agents on two platforms, a CRM-native agent platform (Salesforce-style) and Gemini Enterprise on Google Cloud, plus a few custom ADK agents.
  • Thirty agents today, 150 planned. Operations in the EU and the US.
  • Low governance maturity; the client wants a provider to enable them while they build their own framework and tooling.

Keep any real pursuit's facts in that pursuit's own folder, never in this book or the learning repository.

53.2B1 · Inventory and the tiering model

List the agent archetypes on each platform, decide which axes drive their tiers, and estimate the distribution. Use the fleet-first rule: tiers are derived from axis values by one rule, never decided per agent (Chapter 13). A starting hypothesis to test against the real inventory, Assumption illustrative:

ArchetypePlatformDriving axesLikely tier
Case summariserCRMPII; read-only; internal usersMEDIUM
Goodwill credit or refund agentCRMFinancial write; external customersCRITICAL if autonomous; HIGH with confirmation
Knowledge assistantGemini EnterpriseInternal data; read-onlyLOW or MEDIUM
HR policy assistantGemini EnterpriseSensitive data; employment contextHIGH; check EU Annex III if it touches decisions about people
Procurement agentCustom ADKWrites purchase orders; supplier dataHIGH or CRITICAL

Checkpoint. Every archetype tiered by the rule, with the distribution across tiers and the axis value that would move each one. The first finding is usually how many agents run on someone's personal credentials.

53.3B2 · The catalog cut

Decide which of your controls apply, which change meaning on a platform you cannot instrument, and which new ones the estate needs.

Control familyCustom ADK agentsGemini EnterpriseCRM SaaS agents
Business limits on trusted factsYour PEP and PDPAgent Gateway with a service extension to your PDPPlatform's deterministic steps inside; your gateway on calls that leave it
Approvals bound to an actionYour approval serviceYour approval service, called from the agent's toolsPlatform approval flows; binding to the exact action may be weaker
Idempotency of effectsKeys at your toolsKeys at your toolsThe platform's behaviour; test and document it
EvidenceYour chained recordsYour records for calls through your gateway; platform logs labelled as importedVendor logs, labelled; your records only at your boundary
InventoryYour registryAgent Registry reconciled into yoursVendor inventory reconciled into yours

New controls an estate like this usually needs: detection of agents acting on personal (maker) credentials; approval of connectors and MCP servers before use; configuration drift detection for SaaS agents; and reconciliation of every platform's inventory into one registry of record (Chapter 45).

53.4B3 · The enforcement-point map

For each control and each platform, name the enforcement point: native platform control, gateway, your PDP (OPA, Cedar, or AGT where it can express the rule), or process only. This is the hinge of the capstone, and the tables in Chapter 41 to Chapter 44 are your inputs. Rules for filling it:

  • Native enforcement counts only where it meets the control's contract (trigger, fail-closed behaviour, evidence) and passes the control's falsifiers on that platform (Chapter 24).
  • A probabilistic enforcement point (a model-judged policy, a classifier) may restrict but never authorise (D52); it cannot carry a business limit alone.
  • For the systems that matter most (payments, customer records, HR), add one shared checkpoint you control, which every platform's agents must pass (Chapter 45).
  • Mark where AGT fits and where it cannot, with the evidence from R25 and the version (Chapter 47).

Checkpoint. No cell says "covered" without naming a mechanism, and every "process only" cell has an owner and a compensating detective control.

53.5B4 · The crosswalk

Map the controls to NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act obligations for this client's risk classes, using the crosswalk-as-code practice: dated [Verified] or [Verify] cells, statuses of implemented, mechanism or not-built with partial noted, four numbers in the report (Chapter 48). If the client is regulated, add the sector overlay (Chapter 49). Classify the HR assistant and any agent touching credit or eligibility under Annex III explicitly; those carry high-risk obligations from 2 December 2027.

53.6B5 · Evidence and assurance

For each control, write what evidence it emits, where it is stored, who can read it and how an auditor verifies it. Normalise into one decision schema and label provenance: your chained, signed records against imported platform logs (Chapter 45). Decide retention per class of record, and keep personal data out of decision records where a reference will do (Chapter 21).

Checkpoint. An auditor's walk-through for one CRITICAL action, from the request to the anchored record, with every step reproducible by someone who is not you.

53.7B6 · Operating model and rollout

RACI with one accountable owner per decision, promotion gates by tier, the waiver process with expiry, and a 90-day interim state against a 12–18 month target, each with dependencies and risks (Chapter 50, Chapter 51). With 30 agents growing to 150, the plan must show how the hub stops reviewing every agent: self-service promotion for LOW and MEDIUM by month six is a reasonable target to argue for.

53.8B7 · Build the toolkit

Package a generic core and a client overlay, write the platform adapters your B3 map needs, and ship a conformance suite the client can run. ARB's src/aicoe/ is the reference implementation of the core's ideas; Rebuild rep R10 and your Capstone A code are your own starting points. A layout to start from:

agentgov/                       # generic core: no client facts, ever
  catalog/  schemas/  tiering/
  pdp/rego/  pdp/cedar/         # bundles + parity tests
  pep/  pip/                    # library the adapters call
  evidence/                     # chain, sign, anchor, verify
  gate/  conformance/           # generated, never hand-edited
  registry/                     # record of truth + reconcilers
  adapters/
    geap/                       # service extension, registry reader, Model Armor classifier
    crm_saas/                   # inventory reader, decision-log importer, outbound rules
    adk/                        # chokepoint plugin
agentgov_client/                # overlay: params, tier floors, added controls,
                                #   owners, crosswalk cells, sector overlay
conformance_suite/              # what the client runs: each control fires, per platform

Three rules make it last. Client facts live only in the overlay, and a test fails if the core imports it. Every adapter has a fake and a conformance test, as ARB's platform seams do (Chapter 7). And the conformance suite produces the four-number report itself, so the client's coverage claim is generated, not written.

53.9The framework pack

  1. Executive summary: the recommendation, the 90-day plan, the decisions needed (one page).
  2. The estate today: inventory, tiers, findings (B1).
  3. The rulebook: catalog cut and new controls (B2).
  4. The enforcement architecture: one diagram and the enforcement-point map (B3).
  5. Obligations: the crosswalk summary with four numbers (B4).
  6. Evidence and assurance: what an auditor can verify, and how (B5).
  7. Operating model and rollout: RACI, gates, waivers, interim and target, dependencies, risks (B6).
  8. The toolkit: what is delivered, how to run the conformance suite (B7).
  9. Assumptions and open questions, every one labelled.
