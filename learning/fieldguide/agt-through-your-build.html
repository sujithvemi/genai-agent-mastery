47.1The question every client asks

Generic "Why not just use Microsoft's toolkit?" is the first build-versus-buy question a client, a GTM colleague or an interviewer will ask about a governance build, and it generalises: why not AWS's policy engine, Google's gateway, a vendor's guardrails? The answer that survives a follow-up question is never an opinion about the product. It is a measurement taken through your own build: what the thing is, which of your components does the same job, how the two compare on the same cases, and what that means for adoption, stated with a version number.

This chapter does that for the Agent Governance Toolkit (AGT), the open-source toolkit Microsoft introduced on 2 April 2026, because your reference build already ran the experiment. The method is the point: the same four steps apply to every framework in Part VIII and to every platform feature in Part VII.

  1. Its model in five lines. What the parts are and which one makes decisions.
  2. A mapping table. Its concept, your component (by file), and whether the two are equal, stronger, weaker or absent, with "not tested" as an honest fifth answer.
  3. A measured comparison on the components that matter most, behind a seam, with a parity suite.
  4. A defended answer that obeys a wording rule, and the conditions under which you would recommend it.

47.2AGT's model

Generic AGT is an MIT-licensed toolkit for runtime governance of agents, with SDKs in Python, TypeScript, Rust, Go and .NET. Microsoft's launch post describes seven packages:

PackageWhat it does (vendor description)
Agent OSA stateless policy engine that intercepts agent actions before execution; policies in YAML, with OPA/Rego and Cedar back ends
Agent MeshCryptographic agent identity (Ed25519), secure agent-to-agent communication and a trust score on a 0–1000 scale
Agent RuntimeExecution rings, saga orchestration for multi-step transactions, an emergency kill switch
Agent SRESLOs, error budgets, circuit breakers, chaos engineering and progressive delivery for agents
Agent ComplianceAutomated governance verification, compliance grading and mapping to frameworks such as the EU AI Act, HIPAA and SOC 2
Agent MarketplacePlugin lifecycle with Ed25519 signing and trust-tiered capability gating
Agent LightningGovernance of reinforcement-learning training runs

At Build 2026 (2 June) Microsoft added two pieces to what it calls an open trust stack: the Agent Control Specification (ACS), part of AGT, a YAML format for placing controls at five checkpoints (input, model, state, tool execution, output), and ASSERT, an evaluation framework that turns written policies into test scenarios (Chapter 43).

Packaging moved fast. The Python distribution your build pinned, agent-governance-toolkit-core, went 4.0.0 (30 May 2026), 4.1.0 (11 June) and 5.0.0 (3 August), after a 2.1.0 cited in the build's first scoping; the package is classified 4 - Beta and the project calls itself a public preview that "may have breaking changes before GA". 5.0.0 was still the newest release on PyPI on 21 October 2026. Two vendor claims travel with it and should be quoted as claims: sub-millisecond policy evaluation, and coverage of all ten OWASP agentic risks.

47.3How your build tested it

Your build ARB's E3-S4 spike (docs/architecture/AGT_INTEROP.md, spikes/agt/) asked one bounded question: can AGT be a third PolicyEngine, behind the same Protocol as OPA and Cedar, and pass the same parity suite? The spike was deliberately limited to the policy seam; AGT's identity, sandboxing, SRE tooling and framework adapters were not evaluated. The wiring is the lesson, because it is how you evaluate any engine without letting it near enforcement:

  • Its own environment. AGT runs in spikes/agt/ with its own pyproject.toml and lock file. The governance package does not depend on it and cannot import it.
  • The adapter lives in tests/. tests/agt_policy.py satisfies the PolicyEngine Protocol and returns the same Decision shape, but nothing a deployment runs can construct it. test_agt_is_never_the_enforcement_of_record checks the environment, the package source and the dependencies.
  • One policy per control. AGT names one winning rule per evaluation; your Decision carries every control that refused, which is what parity compares. Evaluating one policy per control yields the set, as the Cedar adapter does with its diagnostics.
  • Default deny with one positive allow rule per policy, for the reason in 47.4.
  • Thresholds from the authored params.yaml. AGT's language cannot read data, so the decision point writes the tier caps and non-refundable states into the rules from the same values Rego reads as data and Cedar receives in context. No number is restated by hand.
  • A fail-closed adapter. A worker failure or error, a reply naming a different set of controls than were loaded, an allowed that is not exactly true, or a request with no action becomes a GBL-DENY-01 denial.

The two controls AGT could express, side by side with Rego:

AGT governance.toolkit/v1

apiVersion: governance.toolkit/v1
name: CTL-0001
description: Refund amount ceiling for the agent's derived tier.
agents: ["*"]
default_action: deny
rules:
  - name: CTL-0001-within-cap
    stage: pre_tool
    action: allow
    condition: "action != 'issue_refund' or $CAP_CLAUSES"

Rego

violations contains v if {
	is_write
	amount_minor > cap_minor
	v := {"control_id": "CTL-0001", "reason": sprintf(...)}
}

The shapes are inverted. Rego collects violations and allows when there are none; the AGT policy denies by default and states the control's condition positively, because a positive allow is the only form whose failure is safe. $CAP_CLAUSES is expanded from params.yaml into one clause per tier.

On CTL-0001 and CTL-0003, AGT agreed with OPA on every case: every golden refund, ten boundary cases, the reads on both agents, a refund at each order's own total for every order in the fixtures, and documents that trip each refund precondition alone. make agt-parity reproduces it.

47.4The gaps, measured

Your build agt_pdp.py probe evaluates seven recorded gaps at the pinned release, and test_the_recorded_language_gaps_are_still_there fails if any outcome changes.

#Written asObserved at 5.0.0Consequence
G1deny when arguments.amount_minor > resource.total_minorAn over-total refund is allowedA comparison needs a literal on the right; field-to-field never matches. CTL-0002 cannot be written
G2allow when action in principal.capabilitiesA capable principal is deniedin takes only a literal list; CTL-0008's capability half cannot be written, so the control is out of scope rather than half-covered
G3deny when not principal.verifiedAn unverified principal is allowedThere is no not; every condition must be positive
G4allow when arguments.amount_minor <= 500A request with no amount is allowedA missing number reads as zero; parity holds only because the PIP always supplies the field
G5deny with a malformed operator (=<)Loads without error; the deny never firesNo load-time validation: a typo silently disables a deny rule
G6allow when resource.status == 'paid or shipped'That status is denied" or " is split before quotes are read
G7allow when action != 'issue_refund'A request with no action is allowed!= on a missing field is true; the adapter refuses a request without an action before AGT sees it

Generic The bigger finding is not what the language cannot say but how it fails when it cannot parse what you wrote. Every policy language has a fail-open mode; the engineer's job is to know it and arrange the policy so that the failure is loud.

Two observations beyond the language: every Python policy entry point in the consolidated 5.0.0 package raises a deprecation warning pointing at the package already installed, so there was no non-deprecated import path at that release; and a comment in AGT's own rule evaluator says "In production, would use a proper expression parser". Neither is a reason to dismiss the project. Both are reasons to pin exactly, re-measure on every bump, and read the source of anything you put on an enforcement path.

47.5What the spike found about your own proof

Your build Switching off AGT's state check and its unresolved-order check left the parity suite green at first. The fixtures could not separate CTL-0003's three rules, because every order in a non-refundable state was also marked non-refundable, so the OPA and Cedar suite had carried the same blind spot since it was written. Synthetic documents that each trip one rule alone now run against Cedar and AGT (D62, T2 addendum item 35).

Generic Parity proves that engines agree on the cases you have, not that the cases exercise every rule. The technique that exposed the gap is mutation: break one rule on purpose and check that some test fails. It is the same idea as the falsifiers in Chapter 24, applied to the test suite rather than the control, and it belongs in any parity effort you run for a client.

47.6Why it was not adopted, and when to look again

Your build D63 closed the option of adopting AGT as the enforcement layer "on evidence at 5.0.0, not by preference": its language could not state two of the four authorisation controls, and a deny condition it could not parse never matched. The spike stays as interop evidence, behind the policy seam, never the enforcement of record. Two parts of the ruling are worth reusing as patterns:

  • A revisit trigger, not a calendar date. A re-spike is due when AGT ships a GA release and the gap test goes red on G1, G3 or G5 at a quarterly dependency bump, meaning an unparseable condition no longer silently never matches. A re-spike re-measures parity; it does not authorise adoption.
  • A wording rule. AGT is a benchmark and a likely partner, so every statement about it is versioned, factual and reproducible: "at agent-governance-toolkit-core 5.0.0, its policy language expressed 2 of our 4 tested refund controls, with full parity on those 2; make agt-parity reproduces it". Never "AGT is unsafe" or "AGT fails open" without the version and the condition.

47.8Where AGT fits behind your seams

Generic "Not adopted as the enforcement layer" is not "not useful". There are five places a toolkit like AGT can earn a role without ever becoming the enforcement of record:

RoleHowCondition
A benchmarkParity runs at each release, as nowPinned exactly; the gap test as a tripwire
A shadow decision pointEvaluate every decision in AGT as well, compare, alert on divergence; never act on its answerDecision-equivalence monitoring in production (Chapter 45)
An exchange formatACS as the portable description of where a control sits, for a client running agents on several platformsOnce ACS has a stable version and reference implementations on the platforms the client uses
Modules you lackPlugin signing, saga orchestration or SRE tooling, each evaluated on its ownA separate spike per module, behind a seam, with falsifiers
A client's chosen stackA Microsoft-centred client standardises on itYour catalog's triggers checked for expressibility first; inexpressible controls kept in a PDP you control

The same table applies, with names changed, to AgentCore Policy, GEAP's access policies and every vendor guardrail: decide which controls the native engine can carry, prove parity on those, and keep the rest in the decision point whose failure modes you know (Chapter 17).

47.9Applying it to a new agent

Generic Capstone A's stage A4 asks you to write the controls of a new agent in Rego, then express the ones AGT can express as AGT policies and run parity (Chapter 52). Done properly it repeats the spike in miniature, and the order of work matters:

  1. Classify each control's trigger: a literal comparison, a field-to-field comparison, membership in a data-held list, a negation, an accumulation over time, or a signature check. Only the first is safe in AGT 5.0.0.
  2. Write each expressible control as default deny with one positive allow, thresholds injected from your parameters.
  3. Run the same cases through both engines; add a single-rule mutation for each policy and check a test fails.
  4. State the result with the version, and keep the inexpressible controls in Rego, named as such.

47.11In the room

In the room
  • "Why not just use Microsoft's Agent Governance Toolkit?" "We ran it behind the same interface as our policy engines. At version 5.0.0 its policy language expressed two of our four refund controls, with full agreement on those two, and a single command reproduces the test. The other two need comparisons the language did not support then, so they stay in an engine whose failure behaviour we know. We re-test when it reaches general availability."
  • "Is it any good?" "Parts of it are promising, and it covers ground we do not: plugin signing, SRE tooling, training governance. We have measured only its policy engine. We would evaluate each other module the same way before relying on it."
  • "Could we standardise on it?" "For the controls it can express, once it is GA and passes our parity suite. For the rest, we would keep a decision point we control in front of the systems that matter."
Check before quoting

Check the current AGT version, its release status (preview or GA) and whether the policy language now validates conditions at load time, supports negation and compares fields. Quote vendor performance and OWASP-coverage figures as vendor claims. The ARB result is specific to agent-governance-toolkit-core 5.0.0 and four refund controls.

Waiting on the builds

When the D63 trigger fires, ARB re-runs the spike; update the gap table, the verdict and the mapping table here from its new AGT_INTEROP.md. If ACS reaches a stable version with a reference implementation for Google Cloud, add a worked example expressing ARB's enforcement points as ACS.

47.12Prove it

Defend cold · R25 · BT16
  1. RecallThe four steps of reading a framework through your build.
  2. RecallWhich two refund controls AGT 5.0.0 expressed, and why the other two could not be written.
  3. ApplyExplain, with the 2×2 in 47.4, why every AGT policy in the spike is default deny with one allow rule.
  4. ApplyDesign the mutation test that would have caught the CTL-0003 fixture blind spot.
  5. DesignA shadow-PDP deployment of AGT next to Rego: where it runs, what is compared, who is alerted, and why it can never act.

Rep: R25 (GF-1), then BT16 (install AGT in its own environment, write one policy, evaluate five cases). Card candidates: how a policy language fails matters more than what it expresses · default deny plus one positive allow · a revisit trigger, not a date · parity proves agreement, not coverage.
