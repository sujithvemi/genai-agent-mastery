F.1How cards are made

Every chapter ends with card candidates: the ideas most worth being able to produce cold. They are candidates, not cards. The Learning OS rule is that only material you have defended (graded B or better in a Defend rep) becomes a card, because a card made from something you only read trains recognition, not recall.

  1. Do the chapter's Defend questions out loud or on paper, then grade yourself.
  2. For each candidate you can now explain, write a card in learning/cards/REVIEW.md: the front is a scenario, a trade-off or a failure mode, never a definition; the back is the key idea and the gotcha, in your words, with the chapter as source.
  3. Review on the deck's schedule (boxes of 1, 3, 7, 21 and 60 days; a pass moves a card up a box, a fail sends it back to box 1).

An example of a candidate turned into a card:

CandidateFrontBack
a fallback model is a different model (Chapter 55)Peak traffic, the primary model throttles, and an engineer proposes switching to another provider's model "just for today". What do you say?A different model is a behaviour change: only a fallback already evaluated and inside the signed-off configuration may serve (model pin and sign-off digest). Otherwise degrade: queue effects, answer reads, never relax the PEP.
