PART IV · CHAPTER 22

The evidence plane

Governance is only as credible as its proof. The evidence plane turns every decision and every act into a record that is minimised, chained, signed and anchored somewhere the writer cannot rewrite, so that an auditor in a fresh process, months later, can verify what happened without trusting the system that did it.

22.1A record, not a log

Generic A log is written for engineers: verbose, mutable in practice, kept as long as storage allows. A record is written for an accountable reader: minimal, attributable, tamper-evident, retained by policy, and verifiable by someone who does not trust the writer. P1's evidence in ARB was an append-only JSONL file, append-only by convention: anyone who could write it could rewrite it, and nothing about the file said whether they had (GAP-11). It also stored tool arguments and results verbatim, names and emails included (GAP-09). That combination, a log that can be falsified and that leaks, is what most agent platforms ship by default.

Four properties turn a log into a record, and each is a separate mechanism:

22.2Controls live in the writer

Your build D21 put minimisation and tamper-evidence inside EvidenceWriter, not at the call sites, with a one-line reason: GAP-09 did not happen because someone decided to log PII, it happened because nothing stopped it. There is no write_raw and no opt-out, so a call site added in a later phase inherits both controls without knowing they exist. A control that depends on every future caller remembering it is a convention.

D21 also fixed the representation: the chokepoint emits tool results as structure, not as a repr string, because field-level classification can never see inside a string. And it made transparent_keys a real control rather than an exemption list: hashing order_id or approver_id would satisfy a naive minimiser and destroy the record, because separation of duties' evidence is "approver identity ≠ executor". A log that redacts its own identifiers protects nobody and proves nothing.

The write order is fail-closed and deliberate. The detail record is written first; if that fails, the whole write fails and the act does not happen. The other order would leave an open record pointing at detail that was never stored. A detail record whose open line then fails is harmless: the detail store is content-addressed, so a retry writes the same address. Chapter 21 covers the minimisation design (D23, D25) in full.

22.3The hash chain

Your build Each record carries the hash of the one before it. The digest covers the record's id, timestamp, kind, payload, its detail-store reference and its predecessor's hash, over canonical JSON (sorted keys, no whitespace) so that two processes hashing the same record agree. The first record's predecessor is the explicit value genesis, not null, so "this is the start" and "someone deleted the previous record" are different states. The writer reads the previous hash from the file on every append rather than caching it, so two writers on one sink (the eval harness and the runtime) still produce one unbroken chain.

The chain's own docstring is careful about its claim: tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. It proves a file was altered; it does not prevent alteration. Preventing it needs a sink the writer cannot rewrite.

22.4Anchors: the end of the chain

Your build A chain has a blind spot at its end: a truncated chain is a valid chain. Delete the last few records (the refund that just happened, and its decision) and every remaining link still verifies (GAP-19). D34 closed it by publishing the head outside the file:

  • An anchor seam with no update and no delete. AnchorSink.publish(anchor) and history(log_id), and nothing else; a test asserts the absence structurally, so adding a delete method is a red test, not a convenience. Publishing is idempotent for an identical anchor (a writer that died after publishing must be able to retry) and refuses a conflicting head at a count already anchored.
  • Bindings by trust domain. In-memory for CI; a local anchor log for one machine, whose docstring says plainly it is not a second trust domain, because a file beside the log is exactly the head pointer an attacker rewrites too; and Cloud Storage, written with if_generation_match=0, so a rewrite fails at the storage layer.
  • The writer publishes (record_count, head_hash) at the cadence authored in params.yaml; the count is read off the file, so several writers sharing a sink anchor the log, not one process's share of it.
  • Two distinct findings. truncated (the log is shorter than an anchor) and diverged (long enough, but a different hash at an anchored index). Every anchor is checked, not just the latest.
  • Silence is reported. Without --anchors, the audit report says the truncation half is unverified, rather than passing quietly.

Two design choices about failure follow from evidence being detective for decisions already enforced live. An anchoring failure does not refuse the act: the record is written, only the external pin is late, and that is recorded. A record-write failure does refuse it (GBL-EVID-01). And the binding never locks its own bucket's retention policy; it reports it, because an application that could lock its own audit store's retention could also have declined to. Locking is a separate, deliberate human act (infra/gcp/lock_anchor_bucket.sh, interactive only, which refuses a bucket with no retention policy). The live report of 2026-10-16 records the lock as exercised: test_the_anchor_bucket_retention_is_locked passed against the real bucket, so the retention policy can no longer be lifted or shortened.

22.5Signatures

Your build A chain alone does not stop an attacker who edits a record and recomputes every hash after it. Signatures do, provided the attacker does not hold the key. Every ARB record is signed; since E4-S3 the signature is Ed25519, with the private key in Cloud KMS in live mode, so verifiers hold only public keys and cannot forge (Chapter 18). Combined with anchors in a separate trust domain, a rewrite must defeat both the signature and the anchor history.

Gotcha, found while writing this chapter

ARB signs every record but, as of this edition, no code path verifies record signatures across processes: verify_chain and verify_evidence accept a verifier, and nothing passes one. The audit CLI is hash-only, and its docstring still explains why with the pre-E4 reasoning (a per-process HMAC key cannot be shared). E4-S3 removed that obstacle. The remaining work is to publish the evidence signing key's public half (as governance/trust/signoff_keys.yaml already does for sign-offs) and have audit verify and the evidence pack check signatures. Until then, the signature half of CTL-0011 is produced but unverified. Logged in Appendix G.

22.6Sealing and unsigned tails

pm-agent pm-agent took signing in a different direction: it signs the chain head rather than relying only on per-record signatures, and it learned two lessons about when to sign.

  • Seal at every boundary (D-PM-P5-f). Signing the head only on demand left an unsigned tail after a normal run. Now every run boundary (close, park for approval, decision, reject, clarify, cancel) and every gate record ends sealed, and an unsigned tail raises an alert visible in gov report.
  • Never sign over an unchecked tail (D-PM-P5-h). Sealing at every boundary created a new hazard: the next session would sign over a pre-existing unsigned (possibly contaminated) tail as soon as it recorded its alert. The fix refuses: a session that starts on a log with records after the last signature stops, unless an explicit acceptance with a reason is recorded. A refused gate or alerts command appends nothing.

The principle: a signature over records nobody checked turns unknown content into attested content. Sign what you wrote; refuse to sign what you found.

22.7What a fresh process can prove

Your build D28 defined the audit CLI by what it can honestly prove from a fresh process:

  • aicoe audit verify walks the chain and, as a separate check, a completeness invariant: every action record has an earlier decision record for the same call, which is the after-the-fact reading of "no action without evidence". With --anchors it checks every anchor for truncation and divergence.
  • aicoe audit trace <refund_id> reconstructs a call from the open log's transparent fields alone; every detail reference shows as a marker unless --reveal-as PRINCIPAL mints a scoped capability and the detail store honours it. The minimal proof is visible to anyone; the personal content only to an authorised principal.

The demonstration to show anyone is make demo-tamper: a governed run verifies; editing one record makes verification fail at that record; truncating the log and checking with anchors fails too.

22.8From evidence to an evidence pack

Your build An auditor or a buyer's risk function does not want a JSONL file; they want the answer to "why is this agent allowed to run?". ARB's evidence pack (aicoe gov evidence-pack <agent>, E1-S3) is one self-contained HTML page per agent, generated, never authored: why the agent got its tier, which controls are required and what enforces each, whether conformance passed on a real run, what the golden set and red-team corpus showed, whether every evidence chain verifies, and who signed off. Every value is read from an artifact: the gate record, the conformance report and the chains its rows point into, the golden run, the red-team ledger, the crosswalk, the sign-off and manifest, the cost and model-identity records. Since GR1 it ends with a section on what the page does not show: the bindings that are faked, the fairness line, and who holds the sign-off key.

pm-agent pm-agent's equivalent, gov report, folds the existing log, recomputes the gate with no new evidence, and verifies the chain and signature, so the report re-decides nothing and every number comes from an event the enforcement plane already wrote (D-PM-P4H-h). Its observability spans are stamped with each evidence record's id, so traces and evidence share keys by construction (D-PM-P5-g).

22.11In the room

In the room
  • "We log everything." Ask: can someone with write access change a past entry without detection, can the newest entries be deleted without detection, and does the log contain personal data? Three yeses mean they have a log, not a record.
  • "How would an auditor check your agent's history?" "From a fresh machine with the evidence, the public keys and the anchor history: the chain shows no edits, the anchors show no truncation, the signatures show who wrote it, and an evidence pack summarises it per agent, generated from those artifacts."

22.12Prove it

Defend cold · pairs with R08, BT08, Capstone A3
  1. RecallThe four properties of a record, with the mechanism and control for each.
  2. RecallWhy the detail record is written before the open record, and why that is fail-closed.
  3. ApplyWalk demo-tamper: which check catches the edit, which the truncation, and what the report says without --anchors.
  4. DesignClose the signature-verification gap in this chapter's gotcha: where the public key lives, how it rotates, what the audit tool prints.
  5. Trade-offPer-record signatures with KMS versus head sealing at boundaries, at 200 tool calls a second.

Card candidates: tamper-evident ≠ tamper-proof · a truncated chain is a valid chain; anchors and their window · controls live in the writer (D21) · sign what you wrote, refuse to sign what you found.
