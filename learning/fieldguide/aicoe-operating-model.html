PART IX · CHAPTER 50

The AICoE operating model

The people, decision rights and rhythm that make governed agents a delivery capability rather than a review queue: what an AI centre of excellence offers, how it is structured as an estate grows, who decides what, how agents and teams earn autonomy, how the centre is staffed and run week to week, and the measures that show a board governance is helping delivery.

50.1What an AICoE is for

Generic An AI centre of excellence earns its place by making good agents fast and dangerous agents impossible. It does that with three products, not with meetings:

  • A paved road. The platform, gateways, templates, evaluation harness and pipeline that make the governed way the easiest way to ship an agent (Parts V and VI).
  • A rulebook. The control catalog, tiering, parameters and crosswalk, owned, versioned and ratified (Part III).
  • Assurance. The gate, the evidence, the red team and the reviews that let anyone check what is true (Part IV).

A centre that offers only a review board becomes a queue; one that offers only a platform becomes a vendor nobody has to listen to. The operating model is how the three products are owned, decided and paid for.

Generic Team Topologies (Skelton and Pais) gives the hub its shape. The road is a platform team, consumed as a product with documentation and service levels. Policy authors and evaluators work as an enabling team, coaching spokes until they can do it themselves. The evidence plane and the decision point are complicated subsystems owned by specialists. Spokes are stream-aligned teams that own their agents end to end, including on-call. The three lines model fits around it: spokes and hub are the first line, risk and compliance functions the second, internal audit the third.

Your build ARB's docs/operating/RACI.md groups the catalog's authored owner_role values under five functions (head of engineering, data governance, CISO, agent owner, platform) and lists four roles the evolution phases added in their own right: FinOps lead (CTL-0020), model risk owner (CTL-0021), AI governance office (CTL-0022, CTL-0023) and responsible AI lead (CTL-0025). A test reads every owner_role from the catalog and fails if the RACI table no longer matches, so accountability cannot drift from the controls it names. That is the pattern worth copying: an operating model whose claims are checked against the artefacts it governs.

50.4Earned autonomy

Generic Autonomy should be granted the way credit is: in steps, on a record, and revocable. It applies at two levels.

  • Per agent and action class. An agent starts by suggesting, moves to acting with confirmation, and only then to acting on its own within limits, separately for each kind of effect. Promotion criteria are measured, not argued: supervised run volume, accuracy against reviewed outcomes, how often people override it, and approval quality (dwell time, rubber-stamping signals; Chapter 19). Demotion is automatic on a trigger. The tier sets the ceiling: under ARB's CRITICAL treatment, human approval of the effects that made an agent CRITICAL is mandatory, so no record of good behaviour removes it.
  • Per team. Spokes that pass the gate first time and keep their agents healthy earn self-service promotion for LOW and MEDIUM agents, with the hub sampling rather than reviewing. HIGH and CRITICAL stay with central review. This is the lever that keeps the hub from becoming the bottleneck the first prediction question points at.

Your build The board's output format is ARB's DECISIONS log: dated, numbered, a verdict, the reasons, what was rejected, and the test that makes it falsifiable. A client's governance board that adopts that format gets an audit trail of its own judgement for free.

Avoid counting reviews held, policies written or agents "assessed". They measure activity, and a board learns nothing from them.

50.8Funding and the service catalogue

Generic Fund the road and the rulebook centrally, because their value is shared and their cost is front-loaded; show back platform and model consumption to the spokes so that cost per task is visible to the people who can change it. Publish what the hub offers as a service catalogue with service levels: intake and tiering within a week, a starter template, policy authoring for new effect classes, evaluation and red team on request, an assurance pack per agent. A catalogue turns "governance" from a gate people fear into a set of things they can ask for.

50.10In the room

In the room
  • "Won't an AICoE slow us down?" "If it is a review board, yes. We set it up as a paved road plus rules that compute, so low-risk agents go through on their own and the experts spend their time on the agents that can move money or affect people. We measure lead time by tier to prove it."
  • "Who is accountable when an agent gets it wrong?" "The agent's owner, named in its manifest, for that agent. The control owners for whether their controls work. The evidence shows which it was."
  • "When can the agent act without a person?" "When its record says so: enough supervised runs, few overrides, good approval quality, and only for actions its risk tier allows. It loses that autonomy automatically if something goes wrong."

50.11Prove it

Defend cold · D45
  1. RecallThe AICoE's three products, and what happens if it offers only one.
  2. RecallWhich ARB controls cannot be waived at CRITICAL, and why.
  3. ApplyWrite the promotion and demotion criteria for a claims-triage agent moving from confirmation to autonomy for payments under a threshold.
  4. DesignAn operating model for 30 agents today and 150 next year on two platforms: structure, decision rights, rituals and sizing.
  5. Trade-offCentral review of every agent against self-service with sampling: where do you draw the line, and what evidence moves it?

Card candidates: road, rulebook, assurance · the tier is derived, not decided · autonomy is earned per action class and lost on a trigger · pair every speed KPI with a safety KPI.
