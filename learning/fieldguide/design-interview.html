The design interview for governed agents

How to run a system design interview, or a design review, for an agent that can act: the seven steps and their time budget, three worked scenarios with numbers (a refund agent at retail scale, procurement with suppliers' agents, an assistant at an EU bank), the twist an interviewer is likely to add to each, and what separates strong answers from weak ones.

55.1The shape of the interview

Generic A system design interview for governed agents follows the standard shape (clarify, requirements, estimates, API, high-level design, deep dives, trade-offs) with one difference: the interviewer is listening for whether control of the agent's actions is part of the design or a paragraph at the end. The strongest answers make governance a requirement in the first minutes, a set of components in the diagram and the subject of at least one deep dive.

Trade-offsWhat you chose, what you rejected, what you would revisitHonest residuals; what you would measure first

55.2Scenario 1 · A refund agent at retail scale

Prompt. "Design a customer-service agent for a large online retailer. It answers policy questions, looks up orders and can issue refunds."

Clarify and requirements

  • Functional. Answer from the policy knowledge base with citations; look up the customer's own orders; issue refunds within policy; escalate to a person otherwise.
  • Non-functional. 500,000 conversations a day, peaks three times the average; first streamed token within two seconds at p95; the run API available 99.9%.
  • Governance. Refunds only on the customer's own orders, never above the order total or the tier's limit; above the limit, a signed human approval; every decision and refund recorded tamper-evidently; EU customers' data processed in the EU. Tier: an irreversible financial write acting on its own makes it CRITICAL under the banding in Chapter 13; with approval above a limit it is still CRITICAL for that action class.

Estimate

Assumption 5 model calls per conversation, 4,000 input and 250 output tokens per call, 40-second runs, 15% of conversations refund, 8 evidence records per run.

High-level design

Deep dives to offer

  • The refund decision. The model proposes; the PEP decides on facts the PIP reads from the order system (owner, total, refundable state, amounts already refunded), with a conditional decrement on the refundable remainder so two concurrent refunds cannot overspend (Chapter 28).
  • Evidence at 139 records a second. Shard the chain by tenant or region, each with one writer, and anchor every shard on a fixed window; verification walks shards independently (Chapter 22).
  • Retries. Whole-turn retries only on throttling and only if no effect completed in the attempt; refunds carry an idempotency key from run and step (Chapter 29).
The twist

"It's Black Friday: five times the traffic, and your primary model provider starts returning 429s." Strong answers: reserved capacity for the baseline with spill-over; the AI gateway's backend pool fails over to a second region or a pre-approved, evaluated fallback model (a fallback is a different model and must be inside the signed-off configuration, CTL-0021/CTL-0022); degrade before failing (answer policy questions, queue refunds for later with the customer told); per-tenant budgets so one channel cannot starve the rest; and never relax the PEP to stay up. Weak answers add retries everywhere or switch models without evaluation.

55.3Scenario 2 · Procurement with suppliers' agents

Prompt. "Design an agent that sources quotes from suppliers' own agents over A2A and raises purchase orders."

  • Requirements. About 2,000 purchase orders a day; approvals above a value threshold; separation of duties between requester and approver; supplier messages are untrusted.
  • Design centre. The buyer agent talks to supplier agents through an agent gateway that verifies signed agent cards against a registry of approved suppliers; supplier content is data, never authority; the PO write passes the PEP, which reads catalogue prices, budgets and supplier status from the buyer's own systems (Chapter 5).
  • Deep dives. Declared edges and provenance envelopes for cross-agent messages (ARB's CTL-0016 to CTL-0019); idempotent PO creation; evidence that spans two organisations (yours is the record of your decisions; theirs is imported and labelled).
The twist

"One supplier's agent is compromised. Its quotes now contain instructions and prices 40% above catalogue." Strong answers: the instructions do nothing because messages carry no authority; the price limit is decided on the buyer's catalogue price from the PIP, so inflated quotes are refused or escalated; anomaly detection is a detective layer that flags the pattern but does not authorise anything; the registry suspends that supplier's edge, which every enforcement point honours; the evidence shows every message and decision for the incident review (Chapter 31).

55.4Scenario 3 · An assistant for relationship managers at an EU bank

Prompt. "Design an assistant that answers relationship managers' questions from internal documents, drafts client emails and logs activities in the CRM."

  • Requirements. Reversible writes only (activity logs, drafts); no credit decisions, which keeps it outside Annex III; EU residency; retention to the bank's record-keeping policy; transparency to recipients where content is AI-generated (Chapter 48).
  • Design centre. Permission-aware retrieval that filters by the user's entitlements before the nearest-neighbour search, regional model endpoints with the location in the approved configuration, the model inventoried for model risk, PII kept out of traces (Chapter 4, Chapter 49).
  • Deep dives. Entitlement filters and their failure mode (post-filtering returns too few results or leaks); residency of every store including caches and traces; how a model change is re-validated.
The twist

"The regulator asks you to reconstruct an answer a relationship manager sent a client nine months ago, with its sources." Strong answers: traces were sampled and expired, so the answer comes from the evidence plane, which recorded the user, the model pin and version, the prompt and configuration digest, the retrieved document ids with content hashes, and the output hash; documents have changed since, so either the index keeps versions or the record keeps hashes that the document store's history can match; anchors prove the records were not altered. Weak answers say "we would check the logs".

55.5What strong answers have in common

  • Effects first. They ask what the agent can change before what it can say.
  • Numbers early. Tokens per minute at peak and concurrency appear before the diagram.
  • Facts from systems. Every limit is decided on data the model cannot assert.
  • One chokepoint per effect class, with evidence written before the effect.
  • Failure as design. Throttling, outages, retries and stuck runs are designed, not apologised for.
  • Platform literacy. They know what the managed services provide and where those stop (Appendix I).
  • Honest residuals. They end by naming what the design does not cover and what they would measure first.

55.7Prove it

Drill · PROVE

  2. ApplyRecompute scenario 1's peak TPM and concurrency with 8 calls per run and 60-second runs.
  3. ApplyDraw scenario 1's design from memory in ten minutes, every box labelled with its job.
  4. DesignRun scenario 2 aloud for 45 minutes with a timer; record yourself; grade against the table in 55.5.
  5. Trade-offScenario 3: separate indexes per business unit against one index with entitlement filters.

Cadence: one scenario a month in the PROVE slot, alternating with Capstone B. Card candidates: effects first, numbers early · a fallback model is a different model · reconstruct from evidence, not traces.
