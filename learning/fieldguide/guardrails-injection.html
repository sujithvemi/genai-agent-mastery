PART IV · CHAPTER 20

Guardrails, classifiers and injection

Prompt injection is not a bug to patch but a property of putting instructions and data in one channel. Detection buys time and evidence; it cannot be the control. What holds is structure: untrusted content demoted to data, acts authorised on facts an attacker cannot write, effects constrained to typed actions, and any probabilistic check allowed only to make decisions stricter.

20.1One channel for two things

Generic A language model receives its instructions and its data as one sequence of tokens. Nothing in the architecture marks which tokens are commands and which are content, so any content the agent reads can try to act as a command. When the attacker is the user, this is direct prompt injection. The harder case, named by Greshake and colleagues in 2023, is indirect injection: the attacker writes into something the agent will read later (a web page, a document, an email, a ticket comment, a tool's description, another agent's message) and never talks to the agent at all.

Simon Willison's framing (2025) gives the risk a shape you can design against. An agent becomes dangerous when it combines three things: access to private data, exposure to untrusted content, and a way to communicate externally (or more generally, to cause effects). Any two are manageable. All three together mean that an instruction hidden in the untrusted content can move private data or cause an effect. Most useful enterprise agents have all three, which is why the problem does not go away by choosing a better model.

20.2Why phrase lists lose

Your build Both builds tried detection seriously, and both learned the same lesson.

  • ARB authored injection patterns in governance/policies/guardrails.yaml. The red team (P5-S2) evaded them by writing the same words in forms that read identically to a person and differ byte-for-byte to a regex (RT-03), and by forging the close marker of the data fence from inside a retrieved document (RT-02). Both were fixed: detection now runs twice, once on the text as it arrived and once on a normalised form; and the fence carries a fresh nonce per wrap, so a body cannot close its own fence. The module's docstring draws the conclusion itself: the detector is a tripwire, not the control.
  • pm-agent went further, through phrase classes, structural rules and paraphrase coverage. After about a dozen fix and re-gate rounds, held-out acceptance stayed at 56% (D-PM-P5-q): each fix fitted the corpus and the next held-out set got through. ADR-0005 concluded that a detector cannot win that race by design, froze the output guard as defence in depth, and moved the defence into structure.

The lesson generalises: a detection rate on your own corpus says little about the next attacker's corpus. When someone reports a catch rate, ask what was held out, who wrote the held-out set, and what happens to the act when detection misses.

20.3Defence in layers

Generic No single mechanism stops injection. The design goal is that the bad outcome needs every layer to fail, and that the layers which carry the guarantee are deterministic.

20.4Fences: wrap or strip

Your build Both builds put untrusted text inside a data fence, and they disagree on one point that is worth understanding.

  • ARB wraps and never rewrites. Retrieved knowledge-base bodies are wrapped in an authored data band with a fresh nonce on both markers, hashed and labelled. The text itself is not edited: "silently editing what a customer or an article said destroys the record and teaches the operator to trust a filter." An attempt to forge the band taints the turn and is recorded.
  • pm-agent strips into typed records. Free-text fields (comment bodies, issue summaries, assignee names) are removed from the structured JSON the planner reads and carried separately as typed, neutralised records joined by key, inside a nonce-delimited data channel declared never to be instructions (D-PM-P4H-a, -g). The structure the planner reasons over contains no attacker-writable prose.

The trade-off: wrapping preserves fidelity and evidence but leaves the text in the model's view, so the fence is only as strong as the model's respect for it; stripping removes the text from the reasoning structure but loses signal the planner may legitimately need, and the neutralisation itself becomes something to maintain. Neither is the guarantee. Both reduce how often the model is steered; what the steered model can then do is decided elsewhere.

20.5Taint, and why a message has no authority

Your build ARB's detector has one consequential effect: a pattern match taints the turn, and the Rego bundle refuses any write for the rest of that turn (it reads input.guardrail.tainted). The write's other controls (preconditions, ceiling, approval) are still in force whether or not a pattern matched, so taint is an extra tripwire, not the only barrier.

D40 refined taint for agent-to-agent traffic, and the refinement is the important part. Refusing every downstream act because an upstream message was tainted conflates authority with availability: it lets an attacker turn off a receiving agent by poisoning anything it reads. D40's resolution is that taint is a mark, not a refusal, because a cross-agent message has no authority to begin with. It can never grant, lift or waive a control, so the receiving agent's independently authorised acts still proceed on their own controls, and a poisoned upstream answer changes no enforced decision downstream. Fail closed on authority, open on availability.

20.6Classifiers that can only restrict

Your build Model-based classifiers (Model Armor, Llama Guard, a judge model) catch things patterns miss. They also read attacker-controlled text, so they can themselves be injected. D31 originally kept classifiers off the path to any decision. D52 narrowed that to the actual risk: a classifier must never sit on the path to an ALLOW, or to any relaxation. A classifier whose only power is to tighten, fully compromised, can over-block, never under-block. Five conditions, all required:

  1. Structural monotonicity. The classifier's output type is closed (PASS_THROUGH or RESTRICT(reason)), and the PEP composes final = max(deterministic, classifier_effect) in the order ALLOW < HOLD_FOR_REVIEW < DENY. A property test checks every combination.
  2. Out-of-set and unavailable are not verdicts. A reply outside the token set, a timeout or an error is unavailable, never inferred as pass. What unavailable does is authored per control (on_unavailable: hold or deny; the schema does not allow pass).
  3. The compromised-model standard extends to the classifier. The red team includes a fully compromised classifier; the control holds if no decision is more permissive than without it. Over-blocking under compromise is an availability finding, measured, never waved through.
  4. Plumbing is the test; accuracy is an eval. The control's test proves the composition with a stub. The classifier's accuracy is reported over a labelled golden set and is never the pass/fail proof.
  5. No classifier gates an allow path. "The classifier says safe, so skip approval or raise a limit" is prohibited outright.

enforcement/classifier_gate.py implements it: deterministic controls decide first; the classifier is consulted once; the final effect is the maximum; a classifier_check record carries the classifier id and version, the verdict token, an input digest and the three effects; a hold opens a review item that only a known, active human who is not the agent's owner can release. The false-hold rate (released over decided) is reported, never thresholded. Two controls use it: CTL-0025 (output content safety, for agents exposed to authenticated or public users, with Model Armor as a backend) and CTL-0026 (semantic groundedness, as a support check on top of the typed answer contract).

20.7Structure beats detection

pm-agent ADR-0005, containment by construction, is the strongest injection defence in either build, and it is the one to recommend to clients. Its four parts:

  1. Typed effects. The model may propose effects only as typed actions from a closed union, each targeting only keys that were actually retrieved.
  2. Eligibility policy. A deterministic, per-project Rego policy decides whether the structured state permits each action (Chapter 17).
  3. Display-only free text. Text that gets written (summaries, comment bodies) is sanitised: no URLs, emails, mentions or markup unless present in the request or a structured field; length-bounded. That closes the exfiltration channel.
  4. Provenance lanes. Each action records whether it rests on the request, a structured field or free text; free-text-only actions are kept out of batch approval and shown with their provenance.

Its proof is designed to be falsifiable: replace the planner with a fully hostile model emitting arbitrary actions drawn from the attack corpus, and require zero attacker-originated executed effects, sanitisation on 100% of written text, and nothing from the free-text lane executing without explicit opt-in. The residual is stated: an attacker can still influence which eligible action is suggested and the sanitised wording, which the human approval with provenance covers. Each new product capability costs one typed action, one eligibility rule, one hostile-planner proof row and one eval.

The first R1 review tested the design and found its weak joint. Eight of fifteen novel steers became structured-lane actions, because the lane decision still depended on the injection fence noticing them. The ruling (D-PM-R1-30) is the sentence to keep: a lane is decided by source type, never by a detector. Only system-of-record fields are structured; any text adopted from a comment or a repository is free text, whatever container it arrives in. Containment by construction only holds if every decision it rests on is also structural.

Generic This is a specific instance of a family of patterns from the research literature. Beurer-Kellner and colleagues (2025) catalogue design patterns that trade capability for provable resistance: action selector, plan-then-execute, map-reduce, dual LLM, code-then-execute and context minimisation. Google DeepMind's CaMeL (2025) goes furthest, separating a privileged planner that never sees untrusted data from a quarantined model that does, with capabilities tracking where every value came from. ADR-0005 records the dual-LLM or CaMeL approach as the documented upgrade for any fully autonomous, CRITICAL profile.

20.8The output side

Your build Injection is not the only thing to guard on the way out. Three output controls in ARB:

  • Grounding and citation (CTL-0015): every citation in an answer must be one this turn's retrieval actually returned. The P1 agent's fabricated [KB-0000] is what it closes.
  • Typed answer contract (CTL-0026): the final answer is requested as a typed object, an answer plus at least one citation from this turn's retrieval, or a fixed refusal text. The tool loop runs first; the typed request is a separate call. A monotone support classifier may then hold an answer whose citations do not support it.
  • Content safety (CTL-0025): for agents facing authenticated or public users, a monotone classifier over the output, with an authored fallback text.

pm-agent's frozen output guard plays the same role for plans: it blocks only on hard signals (a canary, an injection or authority steer, an exfiltration address), quarantines items whose only basis is lifted from untrusted text, and discloses what it did (D-PM-P5-close-1a).

20.9Integrity in the relay

Your build When an agent runs in an out-of-process host behind ARB's gateways, the host carries tool results back to the model, so a host could edit them, for example by injecting instructions into a retrieved article after the chokepoint saw it. D63 closed this cheaply because the gateway holds both ends: the tool gateway records a SHA-256 of each result it returns, keyed by session and tool-call id, and the model gateway refuses a request whose tool-result messages do not match those hashes (missing, altered or invented). Even the gateway's own error text must be passed through verbatim. The general point: wherever untrusted content passes through a component you do not fully trust, bind it to a digest at the point you last trusted it.

20.12In the room

In the room
  • "Does your platform stop prompt injection?" "Nobody's does, as a property of the model. What stops the outcome is design: untrusted content treated as data, acts decided on facts the attacker can't write, actions limited to a typed set, and approvals that show where a suggestion came from. Screening helps and is recorded; it is not what we rely on."
  • "Can we use Model Armor as our guardrail?" "Yes, as a classifier that can only make decisions stricter, with a defined behaviour when it is unavailable, and with its accuracy reported rather than used as proof."
Check before quoting

Vendor guardrail products (Model Armor, Bedrock Guardrails, Azure AI Content Safety, Lakera and others) change detection coverage, languages and pricing frequently. Quote their current documentation, and never quote a vendor's detection rate as a security guarantee.
