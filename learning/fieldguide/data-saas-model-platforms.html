PART VII · CHAPTER 44

Data, SaaS and model-provider platforms

Clouds are not the only source of agents. Data platforms put agents next to the data, SaaS vendors ship agents inside the applications that run the business, and the model providers now sell agent platforms of their own. Each brings its own governance features, and many run where you cannot insert your own enforcement point. This chapter surveys the platforms an enterprise architect meets most often and then treats the hard case directly: governing agents whose runtime you cannot instrument.

44.1Where else agents come from

Generic In a typical large enterprise in 2026, agents arrive by four routes: built on a cloud agent platform (the previous three chapters), built inside a data platform next to governed data, switched on inside a SaaS application, or built on a model provider's own platform. The last three are growing fastest, and they are where an AICoE most often discovers agents after the fact.

The second prediction question: inheriting the user's data permissions covers reads inside the platform, which is valuable and hard to replicate elsewhere. It does not cover writes to other systems through MCP connectors, external network access, or business rules on actions, and Snowflake's own description notes that connectors mean "data doesn't always stay fully inside". Treat a data-platform agent's outbound tools like any other tools: through a gateway, under policy (Chapter 36).

44.3SaaS platforms: Salesforce, ServiceNow, IBM

Generic Salesforce Agentforce. Agents built in Agentforce Builder, with Agent Script mixing deterministic steps and model reasoning, run on Salesforce's platform over its data and permissions, behind the Einstein Trust Layer (data masking, zero retention with model providers and audit, per Salesforce's description Verify). Dreamforce 2026 (announcements dated 28 September 2026) added, among others:

  • Agent Optimizer in the builder and for production session analysis, ranking failure patterns by impact (GA October);
  • voice and conversation testing with simulated customer personas (beta);
  • A/B experimentation across agent variants (beta);
  • Agent Health Monitoring with 16+ built-in metrics (GA) and custom scorers;
  • long-horizon agents with approval checkpoints (GA November 2026);
  • a "Superagent" that orchestrates Salesforce and third-party agents in an "A2A-compliant architecture";
  • deterministic, approved-path back-office agents ("Marshall") that produce identical results from identical inputs (GA).

ServiceNow. AI Agents and the AI Agent Orchestrator run in the Now Platform. The governance product is AI Control Tower, expanded at Knowledge 2026 (5 May 2026) to discover AI across the enterprise (including outside ServiceNow, non-human identities and connected devices), observe agents' reasoning (through the Traceloop acquisition), run risk assessments against new NIST- and EU-AI-Act-aligned frameworks, secure access through a Veza integration that can detect an agent acting outside its permissions and shut it down, measure cost and ROI, and govern MCP transactions through a new AI Gateway; integrations named include AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, SAP, Oracle, Workday, Anthropic and OpenAI. General availability was expected in August 2026 Verify.

IBM. watsonx Orchestrate's agentic control plane (announced June 2026, on AWS and IBM Cloud) adds an agent catalogue with semantic versioning and dependency bundling, runtime policy enforcement and content guardrails, observability and agent analytics, credential-health monitoring and an "agent access" overview of which agents reach which integrations. watsonx.governance continues as IBM's model and AI risk governance product Verify.

Two of these vendors now sell governance for agents beyond their own platforms (ServiceNow's AI Control Tower, IBM's control plane), competing with Microsoft's Agent 365 and the cloud registries for the role of estate-wide control plane. A client will often own more than one; the architect's job is to decide which is the registry of record and how the others feed it (Chapter 45).

44.4The SaaS problem

Generic The first prediction question has an uncomfortable answer: for an action inside Salesforce, on Salesforce data, no enforcement point of yours can sit in the path. You cannot add a chokepoint to someone else's runtime. Governance moves to seven levers:

  1. Configuration. Restrict the agent's actions and permission sets to the minimum; prefer the vendor's deterministic paths (Agent Script steps, approved paths) for consequential actions.
  2. Identity. The agent has its own identity in your identity provider, with narrow connected-app scopes and on-behalf-of access where it acts for users, never a shared integration user with broad rights.
  3. Inventory. Reconcile the vendor's agent listings with your registry on a schedule; ARB's reconciliation runs on exports shaped like a Salesforce agent query, a Power Platform listing and an AgentCore runtime listing, and flags orphans and agents running on a maker's credentials (D59).
  4. Contract. Model versions and change notice, data retention and sub-processors, residency, evidence export and audit rights.
  5. Gateway on every call leaving the boundary. When the SaaS agent calls your systems (by MCP, A2A or API), the call passes your gateway and your PDP with trusted facts, exactly as an external framework host does in ARB (Chapter 35). A message from another agent carries no authority (CTL-0016).
  6. Evidence. Pull the vendor's audit and agent logs into your evidence store, normalise them to your schema and record their provenance; they are weaker evidence than your own chained records, and should be labelled so.
  7. External evaluation. Run golden and adversarial cases against the SaaS agent through its API on a schedule, and after every vendor release.

The third prediction question: when the provider runs the "brain" and your systems sit behind a VPC connection, the contract should cover data retention and training use, the regions where the harness and model run, model version notice, session and log retention and export, incident notification, sub-processors, and audit rights. Architecturally, the same rule holds as for SaaS: calls into your systems pass your gateway and PDP (Chapter 10).

44.8In the room

In the room
  • "How do we govern Agentforce?" "Inside Salesforce, with its configuration, permissions and trust layer, plus contract terms. Everything it does to your other systems goes through your gateway and your rules, and you test it from outside after every release."
  • "ServiceNow says AI Control Tower governs every agent." "It can be your registry of record and risk workflow. It still needs enforcement points in each platform and a gateway in front of your systems; discovery and dashboards are not enforcement."

44.9Prove it

Defend cold · Capstone B
  1. RecallThe seven levers for a runtime you cannot instrument, in order of strength.
  2. ApplyAn Agentforce agent issues refunds through your payments API. Design the controls on both sides of the boundary.
  3. DesignChoose a registry of record for a client with Agent 365, AI Control Tower and AgentCore; define the reconciliation flows.
  4. Trade-offA data-platform agent (Cortex Agents) against a cloud-platform agent reading the same warehouse.

Card candidates: you cannot add a chokepoint to someone else's runtime · govern what leaves the boundary · discovery is not enforcement.
