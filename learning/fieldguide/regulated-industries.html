PART VIII · CHAPTER 49

Regulated industries

Banking, insurance, healthcare and the public sector each add rules on top of general AI law. This chapter turns them into engineering: an overlay that adds controls, sets tier floors, fixes evidence retention and names regulated roles, applied to a generic core. It covers what changed in 2026, including new US model risk guidance and Singapore's final AI risk guidelines, and the common thread that runs through every sector: accountability for the third parties an agent depends on.

49.1What an overlay is

Generic Sector regulation does not need a new governance framework. It needs an overlay: a versioned layer on the generic core that changes four things and nothing else.

  • The catalog. Adds controls the sector requires (independent validation, fair-lending testing, adverse-action reasons, PHI minimisation) or tightens a core control's trigger and treatment.
  • The tiering. Adds tier floors for regulated uses ("any agent that informs a credit decision is at least HIGH") or new axis values.
  • The evidence. Sets retention, immutability, content restrictions and who may read it.
  • The operating model. Names regulated roles (a model risk function, a senior manager, a clinical safety officer), notification duties and review cadences.

Two rules keep overlays safe. They add and tighten; they never remove a core control or loosen a core threshold. And the resolved rulebook records which layer each rule came from, so a change to the overlay re-derives every agent it touches, exactly as a manifest change does in your build (Chapter 15).

Your build ARB's tiering already has the hooks: five axes (data sensitivity, action class, exposure, autonomy, blast radius), highest-wins banding, and a CRITICAL treatment that makes approval controls non-waivable (Chapter 13). A regulated value such as sensitive/regulated data already lifts an agent to HIGH. What the build does not have is a sector layer; Capstone B asks you to package one (Chapter 53).

A sector overlay as data might look like this. Illustrative The ids, uses and numbers are examples, not a client's rules:

overlay: banking-us
extends: core@2026.10
tier_floors:
  - when: "use_case in ['credit_decision', 'credit_limit_change']"
    min_tier: HIGH
controls_added:
  - id: BNK-0001
    title: Adverse-action reasons derived from the decision record
    applies_when: "use_case == 'credit_decision' and action_class != 'read-only'"
  - id: BNK-0002
    title: Independent validation before first deployment and after material change
    applies_when: "tier in ['HIGH', 'CRITICAL']"
evidence:
  retention_years: 7
  immutable_storage: required
roles:
  validator: model_risk_management   # second line, independent of the builders

49.2Banking and capital markets

Generic Banks already govern models, and the first question is whether an agent is one. The answer changed in 2026.

JurisdictionRuleWhat it says that matters for agents
United StatesRevised model risk management guidance from the Federal Reserve, OCC and FDIC, 17 April 2026 (Fed SR 26-2, OCC Bulletin 2026-13), rescinding SR 11-7 and OCC 2011-12Risk-based rather than prescriptive: no mandatory annual validation; validation frequency follows materiality and change; vendor models need understanding plus ongoing monitoring. Generative and agentic AI are carved out as "novel and rapidly evolving", to be governed under broader risk management, with a request for information on AI model risk to follow
United KingdomPRA SS1/23, model risk management principles for banks (effective 17 May 2024)A broad model definition that includes AI; model inventory and tiering; independent validation; a senior manager accountable for the framework
European UnionEU AI Act Annex III (creditworthiness of natural persons, from 2 December 2027); DORA (applies from 17 January 2025)Credit-scoring agents are high-risk. DORA governs ICT risk, incident reporting and the register of ICT third-party arrangements, which now includes model and agent platform providers
SingaporeMAS Guidelines on AI Risk Management, finalised 7 October 2026, effective 7 October 2027 with later sections from October 2028Board oversight, an AI inventory, materiality assessment per use, lifecycle controls, and accountability for third-party AI. MAS will consult on further agentic AI guidance in 2027
CanadaOSFI Guideline E-23, model risk management (revised; effective 1 May 2027) VerifyExplicitly covers AI and machine-learning models, including vendor models, across the model lifecycle

Generic The practical answer to the first prediction question: treat the model inside the agent as a model (inventory entry, validation evidence, monitoring) and the agent's actions as operational risk (controls, resilience, third-party management). The two map cleanly onto things your build already produces:

Model risk expectationWhere it comes from in your design
Model inventory with tier and ownerThe registry of record and the model pin per agent (CTL-0021, CTL-0023; Chapter 10)
Validation before use and after material changeEvaluation gates and red-team results bound to the sign-off digest (Chapter 23, CTL-0022)
Effective challenge by someone independentA reviewer outside the build team signs; separation of duties is enforced, not assumed
Ongoing monitoring and outcome analysisOnline evaluation, drift monitors and control-decision rates (Chapter 31)
Vendor model understandingModel cards, the provider's change notices, your own evaluation on your tasks, identity checks on the served model

Two banking specifics change the catalog. In the US, a credit denial needs specific reasons (Regulation B adverse-action notices), which an agent can only give if the reasons come from a decision record, not from a model's after-the-fact explanation. And broker-dealer record-keeping (SEC Rule 17a-4) is one of the reasons cloud object stores offer compliance-mode retention locks, the same mechanism your evidence anchors use (Chapter 22).

49.3Insurance

Generic Insurance regulators focus on unfair discrimination, explainability of decisions and the data used.

  • EU AI Act. AI used for risk assessment and pricing in life and health insurance is in Annex III, so high-risk from 2 December 2027. EIOPA has published an opinion on AI governance and risk management for insurers Verify.
  • United States. The NAIC Model Bulletin on the Use of AI Systems by Insurers (December 2023), adopted by many states Verify the current count, expects a written AI programme, governance, risk management, third-party oversight and testing for unfair discrimination. Colorado's law on insurers' use of external consumer data adds testing and reporting duties.

Agent use cases (first notice of loss, claims triage, underwriting assistance) mostly read and recommend. The catalog changes come from the decisions they influence: a claims-payment agent is a financial write (CRITICAL by your banding), and any agent whose recommendations differ by group needs the fairness testing your build has found hard to make conclusive. ARB's fairness control CTL-0027 is held, not ratified, because the measured criterion could not separate noise from bias on the available data (D74). That is the honest state of the art, and an insurer should hear it plainly.

49.4Healthcare and life sciences

Generic Two regimes dominate: privacy of health data and safety of clinical decisions.

  • HIPAA (US). Protected health information may flow only to covered entities and business associates under a business associate agreement (BAA), in the minimum necessary amount, with audit controls. For an agent, every service in the data path is in scope: the model provider, the vector store, the observability vendor that receives traces, the MCP servers. HIPAA requires its documentation to be kept for six years; many organisations apply the same period to audit records Assumption.
  • Medical device regulation. Software that informs clinical decisions can be a medical device. In the US, the FDA's guidance on clinical decision support distinguishes non-device support (a clinician can independently review the basis) from device functions, and its guidance on AI-enabled device software, including predetermined change control plans, sets lifecycle expectations Verify current versions. In the EU, the Medical Device Regulation applies, and AI that is a safety component of a device falls under the AI Act's Annex I timeline (2 August 2028).

The overlay is the heaviest of the four: autonomy capped at "suggests" for anything clinical, a clinician as the decider with the basis shown (which is also what keeps support outside the device definition), PHI kept out of prompts, caches and traces wherever possible (Chapter 21), and a BAA, or no PHI, on every hop. A clinical safety case, the healthcare analogue of a sign-off, records hazards and their mitigations.

49.5Public sector

Generic Governments add transparency to the public, rights of appeal and records law.

  • United States. OMB memorandum M-25-21 (April 2025) requires agencies to apply minimum risk-management practices to "high-impact" AI (pre-deployment testing, impact assessment, ongoing monitoring, human oversight and remedies) or stop using it Verify its current status and deadlines. Cloud services used by agencies need FedRAMP authorisation, so check each agent service, not just the cloud.
  • United Kingdom. The Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard is mandatory for central government departments: a public record of each algorithmic tool, its purpose, data and human oversight.
  • European Union. Access to essential public services and benefits is an Annex III area; public bodies deploying high-risk AI must carry out a fundamental rights impact assessment (Article 27); high-risk systems already used by public authorities have until 2 August 2030.

Public records and freedom-of-information laws reach agent conversations and decisions, which turns evidence retention from a choice into an obligation, and sovereignty requirements often decide the platform (Chapter 33).

49.7Third parties: the common thread

Generic Every sector now holds the firm accountable for AI it buys: MAS's guidelines say so directly, DORA requires a register and exit plans, the US banking guidance expects understanding and monitoring of vendor models, HIPAA requires BAAs. An agent multiplies third parties.

49.9In the room

In the room
  • "Does our model risk team need to validate every agent?" "They should inventory the model inside each one and validate in proportion to materiality. The agent's actions are governed by controls we can show firing. For anything that informs a credit decision, we would expect independent review before it goes live."
  • "Can we use this agent with patient data?" "Only if every service the data touches is covered by a business associate agreement, including the tracing tools, and the agent only suggests while a clinician decides. Otherwise we design it so that patient data never reaches those services."
  • "Do we need a separate framework for each regulator?" "No. One core rulebook, plus an overlay per sector that adds controls and tightens thresholds. Each rule records where it came from, so you can show any regulator the part that answers them."
Check before quoting

Sector rules moved quickly in 2026: US model risk guidance was replaced in April, MAS finalised its AI guidelines in October, and US federal AI policy changed status during the year. Check the current text, its effective date and its scope (bank size thresholds, which entities, which uses) before naming any rule in a client document. This chapter is not legal advice.
