E.1How to use the reading map

The book links out wherever someone else has covered a topic better than a chapter can. This appendix gathers those links in one place: first the habits for reading them, then every chapter's further reading in book order, collected automatically each time the book is built, so it never drifts from the chapters.

  • The core shelf. The dozen works the book leans on most are on the reading shelf at the front, with the chapters that use each.
  • Read for the decision, not the topic. Before opening a source, write the question the chapter left you with. Stop when it is answered; mark the rest "later" in learning/resources/REGISTRY.md.
  • Vendor documentation dates fastest. Anything about a platform's features carries the chapter's Facts as of date; re-check before quoting (the register lists what to re-verify, Appendix H).
  • Your own library first. Downloaded guides and saved newsletters are indexed in learning/resources/REGISTRY.md; the radar in learning/radar/ watches what changes.

E.2Papers worth reading in full

Most papers are better skimmed for their figure and their limitations section. These repay a full read for an architect of governed agents:

  • Vaswani et al., "Attention Is All You Need" (2017): the architecture under everything in Part II.
  • Kwon et al., "Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Model Serving with PagedAttention" (2023): why serving capacity behaves as it does.
  • Yao et al., "ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models" (2022): the loop in Chapter 2.
  • Greshake et al., "Not What You've Signed Up For: Compromising Real-World LLM-Integrated Applications with Indirect Prompt Injection" (2023): the threat model behind Part IV's injection boundary.
  • Sculley et al., "Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems" (2015): why agents decay (Chapter 40).
  • Debenedetti et al., "Defeating Prompt Injections by Design" (CaMeL, 2025): control and data flow separated by construction, the research version of your chokepoint.
