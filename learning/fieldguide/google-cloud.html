Google Cloud: Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform

Your AICoE standardises on Google Cloud, so this is the platform to know service by service. In April 2026 Google folded Vertex AI into the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (GEAP) and organised it as Build, Scale, Govern and Optimize. This chapter walks each pillar with its current status, examines the gateway's policy model closely, maps GEAP to your control catalog, and states plainly where it leads and where AWS and Microsoft are ahead.

41.1The shape of Google's offer

Generic At Cloud Next on 22 April 2026, Google relaunched its enterprise AI offer as Gemini Enterprise, in three parts:

  • Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, the developer platform, described as "the evolution of Vertex AI". Google states that "all Vertex AI services and roadmap evolutions will be delivered exclusively through the Agent Platform".
  • The Gemini Enterprise app, where employees discover, create and run agents: a no-code Agent Designer for scheduled and triggered agents, an Agent Gallery of validated partner agents with an IT request-and-approval flow, and an Inbox for managing and monitoring agents.
  • A partner ecosystem of third-party agents (Oracle, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Adobe, Workday among those named).

Names changed with the launch: Vertex AI Agent Engine became Agent Runtime, and the evaluation, memory and session services were renamed under the Agent Platform. Older documentation, blog posts and your own build's early records use the old names (ARB's D32 recorded the rename at the time).

41.2Build

  • ADK is the code-first framework, model-agnostic, now with graph-based sub-agent networks; Google reports more than six trillion tokens a month through it (Chapter 35).
  • Agent Studio is the low-code canvas, exportable to ADK; Agent Garden holds templates (invoice processing, financial analysis and others).
  • Model Garden offers 200+ models: Gemini, Gemma, and third-party models including Anthropic's Claude family. For your pins, the provider-reported model_version is what CTL-0021 checks (Chapter 10).
  • RAG Engine and Vector Search for retrieval; enterprise search over documents, which the documentation now also labels Agent Search (formerly Vertex AI Search).
  • Grounding is a family of options, not one service. The grounding documentation lists Google Search, Google Maps, Agent Search (your indexed enterprise content), Elasticsearch, Parallel and Exa web search, your own search API, RAG, and Web Grounding for Enterprise, a web-grounding variant aimed at regulated customers Verify its data-handling terms before quoting. Grounding attaches sources to an answer; it does not by itself prove the answer used them, which is why your builds check cited ⊆ retrieved (Chapter 4).
  • Model training and inference. Gemini models can be tuned with supervised fine-tuning (text, documents, images, audio, video and function calling), preference tuning and reinforcement-learning fine-tuning, including continuous tuning; open models in Model Garden support supervised and distillation fine-tuning and LoRA/QLoRA; embedding models can be tuned too. Inference runs on the consumption options in 41.3 and on your own endpoints for open models (Chapter 9). A tuned model is a new model: it gets its own pin, evaluation and sign-off, and Security Command Center now reports tuned models that Model Armor does not protect (Chapter 10).
  • Managed Agents API (preview since May 2026): a REST-first API for autonomous agents in a managed sandbox, Google's counterpart to managed harnesses elsewhere.
  • Protocols: A2A (Agent Registry supports A2A 1.0), MCP, A2UI for agent-generated interfaces, and AP2 and UCP for payments and commerce (Chapter 36).
  • Cloud Marketplace is the procurement path for third-party agents: A2A agents bought through Marketplace are added to a Gemini Enterprise app by an administrator, move from procured to integrated, and appear in the Agent Gallery under the visibility the admin sets (Pre-GA as read 2026-10-21). The documentation is explicit that Gemini Enterprise's console Model Armor settings do not protect Marketplace agents; the provider must configure Model Armor in the agent's own code. A bought agent is still an agent in your inventory, with a tier and an owner on your side (Chapter 13).
  • Also: batch and event-driven agents triggered from BigQuery and Pub/Sub, multimodal live streaming, and CodeMender for finding and fixing code vulnerabilities.

41.3Scale

  • Agent Runtime: managed hosting with sub-second cold starts, provisioning in under a minute, long-running operations up to seven days, custom containers, and revisions with traffic splitting (preview) (Chapter 37).
  • Sessions with your own session ids; Memory Bank with automated generation from event streams, revision history and memory profiles (GA July 2026); multi-region and global endpoints (GA June 2026).
  • Sandbox: code execution, plus shell and computer-use sandboxes (GA September 2026), custom containers and snapshots (preview).
  • Consumption: Standard PayGo on dynamic shared quota with spend-based usage tiers, Priority and Flex PayGo, Provisioned Throughput, batch, and the global endpoint (Chapter 9).

Three reading notes on the last three rows, because each is easy to oversell:

  • Agent Compliance checks posture, not behaviour. Compliance Manager evaluates cloud configuration against frameworks (is Model Armor attached, are permissions excessive, is data residency set). It cannot tell you whether a refund above the order total was refused; that evidence comes from your enforcement and evidence planes (Chapter 22). Report the two side by side and label which is which.
  • Agent Anomaly Detection is detective and asynchronous. It finds patterns after the fact, which is valuable for the behaviours no rule anticipated (the catalog-scraping example in Google's announcement is exactly that). Its optional ADK callback that blocks a tool call above a severity threshold is a model-judged restriction: admissible under D52 because it can only restrict, never as the control a deterministic limit should be (Chapter 20).
  • Anomaly Detection needs raw prompt and response telemetry. Your builds deliberately keep arguments and results out of spans and route content through one redaction policy (D26; Chapter 31). Enabling the service means accepting a second store of raw content, in a US or EU multi-region log bucket, with its own access and retention. Decide that per agent and per data class, and record it; it is a data-boundary decision, not a monitoring toggle (Chapter 21).

41.5Policy on the gateway, examined

Generic GEAP has two native policy types, and the difference between them is the most important thing for an architect to understand about the platform.

IAM access policies (unified access policies) are deterministic. Each policy holds rules with an allow or deny effect, deny overrides allow, and there are dry-run and enforce modes. The CEL attributes documented are about the destination: registered or not, the MCP server, method, tool name, prompts and resources, and the tool's annotations (read_only_hint, destructive_hint, idempotent_hint, open_world_hint); for unregistered destinations, host, path and method. Principals are SPIFFE agent identities. The documentation lists no attributes for tool-call argument values or for business facts.

Semantic governance policies are natural-language constraints ("always use UPS for shipments within the USA") that a model evaluates against tool calls and user intent. The documentation says plainly that the service "uses an LLM to implement natural language policies" and that verdicts "may not be accurate"; the sample configuration fails closed; evaluations consume model tokens and are billed.

The prediction questions follow:

  1. "Refund ≤ order total" is not expressible natively in a deterministic GEAP policy, because it needs an argument value and a trusted fact (the order total) read from the order system. You can express it in a semantic policy, but then a probabilistic judge decides a financial limit. The deterministic answer on GCP is to keep your PDP and PIP: either behind your own chokepoint, as ARB does, or behind the gateway's authorization service extension, the programmable hook that Google's governance template uses for custom authorisation (Chapter 16).
  2. Tool annotations are declared by the tool's server. A rule on destructive_hint is only as good as the server's honesty. Verify annotations at registration and pin them with the description (your CTL-0024 hashes name, description and schema; extend it to annotations), or a mislabelled tool walks through a "deny destructive" rule.
  3. A model-based policy may only restrict (D52). Semantic governance fits that rule when it can only deny and fails closed, and when it never stands in for a deterministic limit. Use it for business-intent constraints that cannot be written as code, layered on top of deterministic controls (Chapter 20).
Compare

AWS AgentCore Policy evaluates Cedar over the tool call, including its input arguments, deterministically, with default deny and forbid-wins (GA March 2026) (Chapter 42). Even there, a rule such as context.input.amount < 1000 checks what the agent asserted; a limit against a trusted fact still needs a PIP. No platform supplies the PIP for your business systems.

41.6Optimize

  • Evaluation through the Gen AI evaluation service: offline evaluation, online monitors on live traffic, multi-turn autoraters that evaluate whole conversations, failure clusters and quality alerts.
  • Simulation: synthetic users and virtualised tools, scoring task success and safety.
  • Observability (GA June 2026): dashboards and traces, a unified trace viewer, an App Topology API, ADK telemetry metrics.
  • Optimizer: clusters real-world failures and proposes refined system instructions and tool descriptions; treat its output as a candidate change (Chapter 39).
  • Also: Example Store for few-shot examples, and a feedback service (preview).

41.11In the room

In the room
  • "Doesn't GEAP govern agents for us?" "It supplies strong enforcement points: identity, a gateway, access policies, Model Armor, a registry. The business rules that need your data, the approvals, the tamper-evident evidence and the release gate are still yours, and they plug into those points."
  • "Can we enforce our refund policy in the gateway?" "Deterministically, through a service extension that calls your policy engine with facts from your order system. The natural-language policy feature is a useful extra layer, not the limit itself."
